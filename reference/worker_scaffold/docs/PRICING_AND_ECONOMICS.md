# Pricing and economics

## Decision

Use fixed recurring SaaS tiers, not pay-per-client.

### Why
Performance billing sounds easy to sell but creates four permanent operational liabilities:
1. Who caused the booking?
2. Did the customer actually attend?
3. What revenue did the customer generate?
4. Is the merchant under-reporting to reduce the bill?

A flat plan turns all four into analytics questions rather than billing disputes.

## Founder cohort

First 5 businesses:
- 990 MAD setup
- 990 MAD/month
- 60-day minimum
- business owns and pays its Meta WhatsApp usage directly
- founder rate in exchange for permission to publish anonymized metrics and request a testimonial

This is deliberately easier to close than the target price. Do not optimize first five customers for MRR; optimize them for proof, automation reliability and sales assets.

## Standard plans after proof

| Plan | Monthly | Booking allowance | Best fit |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Core | 1,490 MAD | 250 | solo/very small appointment business |
| Pro | 1,990 MAD | 750 | busy salon/studio/clinic |
| Multi | 2,990+ MAD | custom | multi-location or multiple teams |

Setup: 1,490 MAD standard.

## Value test

A 1,990 MAD subscription needs to be a small fraction of protected or enabled gross profit. Do not sell Pro to a merchant whose economics cannot support it.

Example: 200 monthly appointments x 150 MAD average ticket = 30,000 MAD booked revenue. A 1,990 MAD fee is 6.6% of gross booked revenue before any no-show recovery, repeat bookings or operational savings.

## Billing risk rule

Never bundle WhatsApp usage into our fee at the beginning. The merchant should connect its own Meta business account and payment method. That makes messaging a pass-through infrastructure cost we do not finance.
